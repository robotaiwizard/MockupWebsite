The House

Old soul, new spirit.

To us, unleash means opening a door. Behind a timeless facade on a quiet street below Strefi Hill, you find a modern, living Athens.

  1. 1930s

    A new house in a growing city

    Athens in the 1930s was a city on the move. After 1922 it had grown fast, and new homes filled the streets climbing towards Strefi and Lycabettus. Our house went up on Koumarianou in that decade, in the neoclassical style Athenians had loved for a century: symmetrical windows, crisp cornices and tall shutters.

  2. The decades after

    A family home

    For most of the century it was lived in floor by floor, its marble staircase worn smooth and its mosaic floors polished by generations of feet, while the neighbourhood around it filled with bookshops, theatres and tavernas.

  3. The restoration

    Brought back to life

    We restored the facade, cornices, shutters, marble stair and mosaic floors, and kept them in plain sight. Inside, we started again: six contemporary rooms of oak, linen, stone and light, with new rooftop rooms set back above the old cornice.

Unleash the City

Our Athens, curated.

The places we send our friends. Everything here is walkable from the house, or a short metro ride; walking times are approximate.

  • ~5 min walk

    Exarchia & Kallidromiou

    Right on our doorstep. Record shops, bookstores and rakadika, plus the Saturday farmers' market on Kallidromiou Street, one of the best laiki in the city.

  • ~2 min walk

    Strefi Hill at sunset

    Our neighbourhood hill: pines, stone steps and rocks where locals gather as the Acropolis turns pink and Lycabettus lights up across the rooftops.

  • ~10 min walk

    National Archaeological Museum

    One of the world's great museums and practically our neighbour. Look for the Mask of Agamemnon, the bronze Poseidon of Artemision and the Antikythera Mechanism.

  • ~35 min walk · 15 min by metro

    The Acropolis

    The Parthenon, the Erechtheion and the Propylaia. Arrive when the gates open at 8am, before the tour groups and the heat. Book a timed ticket online in advance.

  • ~40 min walk · metro to Akropoli

    Acropolis Museum

    A bright modern museum with glass floors over an excavated ancient neighbourhood. The top-floor Parthenon Gallery is aligned with the temple itself. Friday evenings are open late.

  • ~20 min walk to the trailhead

    Sunset on Lycabettus

    The highest point in central Athens. Walk up the pine path through the trees, or take the funicular from Kolonaki, and watch the sun set behind the Acropolis.

  • ~30 min walk

    Plaka & Anafiotika

    Neoclassical mansions, Byzantine churches and, tucked under the Acropolis rock, Anafiotika: whitewashed island-style houses built by stonemasons from Anafi in the 19th century.

  • ~20 min walk

    Varvakios Central Market

    The loud, wonderful heart of Athens' food scene, with fish, meat, olives, spices and cheese. Come in the morning, then have grilled fish in one of the market tavernas.

  • ~25 min walk · metro to Monastiraki

    Monastiraki & Psyrri

    Browse the flea market and antique stalls, eat souvlaki on Mitropoleos Street, then lose yourself in Psyrri's lanes of street art, mezedopoleia and late-night bars.

  • ~30 min walk

    Ancient Agora

    Where Socrates walked and Athenian democracy was argued over. The Temple of Hephaestus here is one of the best-preserved ancient Greek temples anywhere.

  • ~20 min walk

    Syntagma & the National Garden

    Watch the Evzones change guard at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier every hour, with the full ceremony on Sundays at 11am, then wander the shady paths of the National Garden.

Our neighbourhood hill

Strefi Hill

Lycabettus gets the postcards, but locals climb Strefi. It's a small, wooded hill of pines, cypresses and rock right in the middle of the city, with a view that takes in the Acropolis, Lycabettus and the whole basin of Athens out to the sea.

What it's like today

Paths and stone steps wind up through the trees from every side of the hill. Near the top you'll find rocky outcrops where people gather at sunset, an open-air amphitheatre used for concerts and neighbourhood events, a basketball court and a small café with tables under the pines.

Go early in the morning for a quiet walk with only dog-walkers and runners for company, or at golden hour. It's a two-minute walk from our front door.

A short history

  1. AntiquityStrefi is one of the low limestone hills of the ridge that runs through Athens and rises to Lycabettus and Tourkovounia. In antiquity this was open countryside outside the city walls.
  2. 19th centuryAfter Athens became the capital of the new Greek state in 1834, the city grew quickly. The hill came to be owned by the Strefis family, who gave it its name. Its rock was quarried for building stone for the expanding city.
  3. 20th centuryNeighbourhoods such as Neapoli and Exarchia filled in around it while the hill itself stayed undeveloped. It later passed into public hands, was planted with pines and cypresses and was laid out with paths, steps and lookouts as a park.
  4. TodayStrefi is one of central Athens' most loved green spaces, and it remains a neighbourhood park rather than a tourist sight.

Getting here

From the airport
Metro line 3 to Syntagma, change to line 2 for one stop to Panepistimio, then a 15-minute walk or short taxi ride. A taxi direct from the airport takes 35–50 minutes.
Nearest metro
Omonia and Panepistimio (lines 1 & 2) are both about 15 minutes on foot.
From Piraeus port
Metro line 1 to Omonia, then walk up through Exarchia, about 45 minutes in total.
Arriving by car
Street parking is very limited. We can recommend a garage nearby.
